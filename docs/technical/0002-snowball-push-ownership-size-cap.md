# 0002: Snowball push, network ownership, collision groups and zone optics

**Status:** Accepted with merge of PR #89.

First written for the ball prototype (#3, PR #7, never merged on its own). Issue #33 (`7.3-ball-push`) supersedes PR #7: it moves the prototype into the multi-place layout (ADR 0004) and reworks it to concept 7.3 and ADR 0003 rev. 2. This file records the result; the PR #7 version is in that PR's history.

## Context

The game depends on one growing, rolling ball per player staying stable in Roblox physics. Concept 7.3 fixes: the character pushes the ball, the ball sits directly in front of the character in walking direction and rolls along, steering is walking (no ball button, so the touch joystick alone steers), no jumping in the Simulator, the server computes the size, and balls of different players do not collide (✅). Ball physics, animations, camera details and the Visual Cap are Development's call (7.3 Spielraum).

Pure collision pushing (the character walks into the ball) breaks down once the ball is much bigger than the character, and PR #7's force only pushed while the ball was already in front, so after a turn the ball stayed behind. Large parts are also prone to jitter and tunnelling, and ball size is the core progression value, so it must not be decided by the client.

## Decision

**Layout (Simulator only).** `src/places/simulator/server/` holds `BallService` (spawn, ownership, collision groups, size) and `MovementService` (walk speed, no jump). `src/places/simulator/client/` holds `BallController` (hold and roll), `CameraController` (auto zoom, push past the own ball), `CollisionController` (local collision groups) and `MovementController` (jump state off). Each keeps its rules in a pure child module tested with `lune run tests`: `BallService/BallCore`, `BallService/CollisionSetup`, `BallService/RecoveryRules` (#94), `BallController/HoldMath`, `CameraController/CameraFit`, `CollisionController/CollisionAssign`. The Studio switches are in `BallService/StudioSwitches`, part creation and spawn placement (look, material and spawn ray from `Config.Ball`) in `BallService/BallPart`. PR #7's `BallConfig` is merged into `Config/Ball`; zone optics stay in `Config/Zones` and `Formulas.Zones.zoneOptics`. The `SimulatorSkeleton*` placeholders are removed. Lobby and Arena are unchanged and keep Roblox default jumping.

**Holding the ball in front (client).** `BallController` puts a sideways-only `VectorForce` on its own ball and sets it every `PreSimulation`. Gravity and ground friction make the ball roll. The controller works in polar coordinates around the `HumanoidRootPart`:
- the hold direction is the last walking direction (`Humanoid.MoveDirection`), so steering is walking;
- the distance is pulled to `radius + HoldGap`;
- the bearing swings towards the hold direction at a limited angular speed (`HoldTurnRateDegrees`, at most `HoldMaxOrbitSpeedFactor × WalkSpeed` sideways, and never more than `HoldCentripetalShare` of the acceleration limit on the curve). A ball that ends up behind the character therefore travels around it, never through it, and a big ball swings slower;
- the character's velocity is fed forward, so the ball keeps pace and push speed follows `Humanoid.WalkSpeed` (base `Config.Ball.WalkSpeed`; the Walk speed upgrade #36 and the "Schnelle Schuhe" pass #60 only change WalkSpeed later);
- desired speed is capped at `HoldMaxSpeedFactor × WalkSpeed`, acceleration at `HoldMaxAcceleration`; force = acceleration × `AssemblyMass`, so every size responds the same. Without a living character the ball brakes.
- The loop reads cached references and works on numbers; it allocates no tables per frame.

**Network ownership.** `BallService` calls `SetNetworkOwner(player)` on spawn and after every server-side move or resize. The owner simulates the ball, so holding it needs no remote. There is no remote in this feature.

**Server-authoritative size.** The client never sends a size, distance or position. Every `SampleInterval` the server samples each ball and grows a **size fraction 0..1 within the zone**, the single interface the optics read. How it grows is in "Growth" below. Ball size is not saved (concept 6.5). Start asserts keep `WalkSpeed < MaxCountedSpeed` (base pushing counts fully; replaces PR #7's `PushMaxSpeed < MaxCountedSpeed`), `GrowthRange > HoldGap`, `VisualCap ≤ 2048` and a valid `DefaultZone`.

**Zone optics.** Visual diameter = `zoneOptics(zone) × (OpticsStartFraction + (1 − OpticsStartFraction) × fraction)`, with `zoneOptics(zone) = min(4 + 0.94 × (zone − 1), VisualCap)` studs. Zone 1 goes from 1.6 to 4 studs. The part is the visual ball. It is resized only when the diameter changed by `ResizeStepFraction` of itself (and for the last step at 0 and 1), and only while grounded; the centre moves by the change in radius in the same step so the solver does not push a penetrating ball out. A whole zone takes about 47 resizes over about 40 s instead of one per sample. PR #7's "×N" label is removed.

**Current zone.** There is no ZoneService and no zone in the World 1 map yet. `BallService` holds a zone per player, default `Config.Ball.DefaultZone` (1). `BallService.SetZone(player, zone)` (validated with `Formulas.Zones.isZone`) is the hook for ZoneService; `GetZone`, `GetSizeFraction` and `GetSizeMetres` read it.

## Growth (issue #34, concept 7.4)

Modules: `BallService/GrowthRules` (pure: metres, credit, block reasons, collision, knock-over), `BallService/ObstacleRules` (pure: contact distance, what a touch means), `BallService/GrowthModifiers` (the upgrade seam), `BallService/BallStep` (one sample of one ball), `BallService/Obstacles` (tagged obstacles, knock-over), `BallService/BallAttributes` (published size), `SurfaceService` (tag lookup) with its pure `TrackGrid`, and the client `BallGrowthController`. There is still no remote.

**Size.** Fraction 0 is the start size (`StartSizeFraction`, 10 % of `Formulas.Zones.maxSize(zone)`), fraction 1 the maximum, metres are linear between them. The optics keep reading the fraction, so the visual cap (`VisualCap`) and metres diverge on purpose.

**Growth rate.** A full zone is `timeToMax × WalkSpeed` studs of normal snow (640 at level 0), so `fractionPerStud = 1 / (timeToMax × WalkSpeed)` (Q-G, ADR 0003). Growth is proportional to counted speed up to `MaxCountedSpeed`. Deep snow multiplies by `DeepSnowGrowthMultiplier`.

**Calibration (AC1).** The interim growth, configured for 41.7 s, measured 47.1 s by hand and 47.4 s by the playtester, which is 14.1 studs/s counted against 16 walked. Nothing in the server maths loses 12 %: `MinDistancePerSample` (0.05) is far below a sample (1.6), and the ground ray does not fail on flat ground. The likely cause is the per-sample limit `min(distance, MaxCountedSpeed × elapsed)`. The position of a client-owned ball reaches the server in packets, not continuously. A sample that saw no new packet counts nothing and the next one sees two packets' worth, which the limit (2 studs per 0.1 s) cuts off; each server resize (47 per zone) adds a short stall that feeds the same loss. A headless model of this reproduces the measurement: 20 packets/s with ±20–40 ms jitter and a 0.15 s stall per resize gives 46.5 to 48.4 s for the old rule against 41.7 s ideal. **This is a model that matches the two measurements, not a proof: Studio was not available to this change.** Replacing the limit per sample by a credit removes the cause: `CountedBurstWindow` (0.5 s) of `MaxCountedSpeed` is refilled over time and spent by counted distance, so bursts count in full and the long-run limit stays `MaxCountedSpeed`, however the owner times its packets. `GrowthRules.spec` runs the same packet model. `MeasuredTimeToMax`, `RolledStuds`, `CountedStuds` and `GrowthSeconds` tell in a playtest whether a remaining deviation is the count or the ball's real speed. A real ball speed of 16 studs/s is assumed. Below 14.9 studs/s AC1 would fail (640 studs in more than 43 s), and the fix would be a calibrated reference speed instead of `WalkSpeed`. `GrowthCounts` flickers to false on samples that saw no new packet (`NotRolling`); that distance is counted by the next sample.

**What counts.** A sample grows the ball only if, in this order (the first failure is published as `GrowthBlock`): not `Paused`, not `AtMax`, a living character exists (`NoCharacter`), the character is in range (`TooFar`), both samples grounded (`Airborne`), the surface is snow (`NoSnow`), not on the own track (`Track`), and the ball moved at least `MinDistancePerSample` horizontally (`NotRolling`). Distance is horizontal: a resize moves the centre vertically, and that is not rolling.

**Character range (AC4).** The root part must be within `GrowthRange` (6 studs) of the ball's **edge on the ground plane** and within `GrowthVerticalRange` of the ball's top or bottom. The true distance to the sphere grows with the radius for a character standing beside it (at the 50-stud ball about 9.4 studs at the hold distance), so no single number would work; the distance to the edge on the ground plane is `HoldGap` at every size. 6 studs is 4× `HoldGap`: room for turning and for character and ball arriving in separate packets, while walking away leaves it in well under a second. Nothing asks whether the character moves, so a ball coasting after a push grows while the character stays in range.

**Surfaces (ADR 0003 D3).** One ray straight down from the ball's centre; `SurfaceService.classify` takes the first tag in `SurfacePriority` on the hit part (`Snow`, `DeepSnow`, `Ice`, `Grass`). Ice, Grass and untagged ground give no growth. Tags are on BaseParts, so a patch may be a non-colliding part lying on the ground (`RespectCanCollide` is false on the ground ray), which keeps the physics flat.

**Track.** Per player, logical, on the server, and never touches geometry (`TrackGrid`). Cell size `TrackCellSize` (4 studs). A cell becomes track when the contact point **has left** it, so the cell under the ball is never its own track and walking straight is fresh snow all the way. `TrackCellHysteresis` (0.5) keeps a walk along a cell border from flipping between two cells: spawn is at x = 0, exactly a border. Track expires `TrackRegrowTime` after the cell was last rolled. Only the contact line is track: a ball touches the ground at one point, and marking the width of a 50-stud ball would mark the snow in front of it and stop its growth, so a huge ball can avoid its own track by moving a few studs sideways. `TrackMaxRolledPerCell` (8) stops rolling back and forth inside one cell from growing forever. How the track looks is not built (7.4 Spielraum), and nothing is rendered per player.

**Obstacles.** Tagged `Obstacle`, BaseParts. The server measures contact from the replicated ball position (`GetPartBoundsInRadius`, then the exact distance to the box), because `Touched` on a client-owned part is reported by that client. Contact margin `CollisionContactMargin` (0.25). A new touch is one collision, and pressing against the same tree is not a steady drain; the first sample after the ball was placed only records contact, and every placement also starts `CollisionProtection`, because that first sample may not see the contact yet and a contact first seen while protected is remembered without a loss. A collision costs `size × (1 − loss)` of the current metres, never below the start size, then `CollisionProtection` (1 s). A resize that follows uses the normal step.

**Knock-over.** An obstacle with the number attribute `KnockOverSize` (studs) is knocked over without loss when the ball's visual diameter is at least `KnockOverSizeRatio` (3) times that size. Studs against studs, because metres and studs diverge under the visual cap. It is taken out of the world for everyone and returns after `KnockOverRespawn` (30 s), or `KnockOverRetryInterval` later while a ball is where it would return. An obstacle without the attribute (or with nonsense) is never knocked over.

**Upgrades.** `GrowthModifiers` is the one seam. It reads `Formulas.Upgrades.effect` with level 0 from `Config/Upgrades` (`growth.Start` 40 s, `stability.Start` 10 %) until UpgradeService calls `setLevelSource`. `Config.Ball.TimeToMax` and `CollisionSizeLoss` are removed, because they would be a second source of truth. Level 100 is 8 s and 2 %.

**MAX and the size display.** Published attributes on the Player: `BallSizeMetres`, `BallSizeFraction`, `BallIsMax`, and `BallScaleAnchor` (Vector3 of the nearest `ScaleAnchor`-tagged instance, set when the ball reaches MAX, because the anchor itself may be streamed out on the client). On the ball: `SizeMetres`, `IsMax`. Attributes are written at most every `PublishInterval` (0.25 s) or on a change of the MAX state or a resize. `BallGrowthController` builds everything for the local player only (ADR 0003 R2): a top-centre size label with `NumberFormat.size`, "MAX!", a glow (`PointLight`, `Highlight`) on the own ball, and an arrow from two handle adornments (no asset, no physics). Foreign balls get nothing. `Config.Ball.MaxSoundId` is empty, so no sound plays yet.

**Assumptions (concept is silent).** Size loss never goes below the start size (DM). A knock-over is global, not per player (7.4 names per-player rules only for the track). Pressing against one obstacle is one collision. Obstacles are box-shaped for the contact test, so a tagged cylinder or sphere counts by its box.

**Recovery (does not get stuck, issue #94).** Modules: `BallService/RecoveryRules` (pure: candidates, support, clearance, bound, stuck timer), `BallService/SafeSpot` (rays and overlap queries), `BallService/Recovery` (placement, bound, park, log) and the Studio-only `BallService/TestGeometry` and `BallService/PlaceBehind` (switch `BallPlaceBehindObstacle`). The server places the ball on spawn and on every respawn, and puts it back (counted in `Recoveries`) when it falls below `ResetBelowY` (`fell`), when the part is gone (`missing`), when it stays more than `LeashDistance` beyond the hold distance for `LeashTime` (`out of reach`), when it is stuck (`stuck`, below) and on the Studio switch (`switch`). Where it goes is decided from the character's replicated root part and the world only; no client value is read. All numbers are in `Config.Ball` and are proposals until the owner confirms them after the playtest.

*Cause of the `fell` loop in run A of #33.* Proven by the code: the put-back position was always `holdDistance` straight in front of the character (`BallPart.spawnPosition`). If the ground ray found nothing there, the ball was put at `root.Y − SpawnFallbackGroundOffset + radius`, in the air, at the same spot. It fell below `ResetBelowY` and was put back at the same spot again, with no check and no limit, as long as the character stood within the hold distance (26.5 studs at diameter 50) of the edge, facing out. Inferred, not proven without Studio: the intervals in run A (4 recoveries within under 2 s) are shorter than a free fall from rest to `ResetBelowY` (about 1.1 s). That fits a ball that keeps its downward speed through the server move (the owner simulates the part), or the server reading a position packet the owner sent before the move. The character's position at the burst is unknown (it ended 74 studs from the edge).

*Safe-position rule.* Candidates lie on circles around the character: straight in front first, then every `RecoveryBearingStepDegrees` (30°) alternating left and right up to straight behind, each at the hold distance and at `RecoveryRingSteps` (+0, +3 studs; the edge then stays within `GrowthRange`). The first candidate that passes all checks is used:
- ground below its centre, found with collidable parts only (`RespectCanCollide`), and not the top of an `Obstacle`. The centre goes radius / (ground normal Y) above the hit (`RecoveryRules.restingHeight`), so on a slope the ball touches the slope instead of reaching into it (#94 round 2, see "Slopes");
- ground at four support points `RecoverySupportShare` (0.5) of the radius away, each at most `RecoverySupportSlope` × that offset (45°) above or below the centre's ground, so the ball does not sit on the rim of a cliff or hole;
- no `Obstacle` closer than radius + `RecoveryObstacleClearance` (0.5, above `CollisionContactMargin`, so the first sample sees no contact, AC9), and no plain block part reaching more than `RecoveryPenetrationTolerance` (0.05) into the ball. Wedges, meshes and terrain cannot be measured by their box without false hits on slopes; the rays are their only check;
- the character is in growth range of the ball there (`GrowthRules.inGrowthRange`), so ground far below a cliff edge is not a put-back spot.
If no candidate passes, the ball's last safe position is checked again at the current size: the last sample where it was grounded twice in a row, touched no obstacle and had the living character in range. If that fails too, the old placement in front is used, with a `warn` (`Unsafe`). `RecoverySpot` shows which rule was used (`Front`, `Around`, `LastSafe`, `Unsafe`). A ball put beside or behind the character stays there until the player walks; the next walking direction swings it in front again (see "Client hold after a recovery").

*Client hold after a recovery (#94 round 1).* The playtest at `f95545a` found a second loop: the server put the ball back `Around` correctly, but `BallController` still held the last walking direction (out over the edge) and swung the ball back over it within about 5 s, while the character stood still; the same swing pushed an `Around` ball against `LargeObstacle` (AC9 observation). Now the client follows the ball after every recovery: when the ball's replicated `Recoveries` attribute changes (only the server writes it, on every recovery, also on live servers), and when a new own ball part appears, the hold direction is set to the bearing from the character to the ball every frame for `RecoveryHoldResetTime` (0.5 s), as long as the player does not walk. A window instead of one frame, because the new position and the attribute may arrive a few frames apart. Walking always wins (`HoldMath.nextHeldDirection`): a player who walks out over the edge again pushes the ball over again, which is a normal fall and counts towards the bound. The client sends nothing and decides no position; it only stops steering the ball away from where the server put it.

*Grace.* For `RecoveryGraceTime` (0.5 s) after every placement a position below `ResetBelowY` is ignored (that sample is skipped), because the owner's packets may still carry the old position. A real fall from ground level to `ResetBelowY` takes about 1 s.

*Bound.* At most `RecoveryBurstLimit` (3) recoveries within `RecoveryBurstWindow` (5 s). The next one in the window still uses the rule above, then anchors ("parks") the ball for `RecoveryParkTime` (3 s) and warns `[BallService] <name> recovery bound: 4 recoveries within 5 s, ball parked at (x, y, z) (<spot>) for 3 s`. While parked the sample does nothing else (`GrowthBlock = Parked`, `Parked = true`): a parked ball cannot fall, so no recovery follows from it. Afterwards it is released where it is and ownership goes back to the player. The switch counts too, so the bound can be reached on purpose in a test. Every switch, respawn and placement goes through the same rule.

*Loop bound (#94 round 1).* The burst rule misses a loop of a few seconds per round (5 s in the `f95545a` playtest: `recent=1` each time). A second rule looks at the place: a recovery whose put-back position lies within `max(RecoveryLoopRadiusMin, diameter × RecoveryLoopRadiusPerDiameter)` studs (10, 1 → 50 studs at diameter 50) of `RecoveryLoopLimit` (3) earlier put-backs within `RecoveryLoopWindow` (40 s) is past the bound. It parks exactly like the burst rule and warns `[BallService] <name> recovery loop bound: <n> recoveries within 40 s and <r> studs, ball parked at (x, y, z) (<spot>) for 3 s`; if both rules hit, the burst warning is printed. With these values a loop of up to about 13 s per round (40 s / 3) is parked at its 4th recovery (15 s into a 5 s loop). Only the same place counts, so a player who loses the ball now and then in different places stays below it; one recovery at the same place every 14 s or more stays below it too. A player who deliberately pushes the ball off the same edge every few seconds is parked from the 4th push: the server cannot tell that apart from the loop, and parking only holds the ball for 3 s. Normal play has no recoveries at all. Values are proposals (owner decision of 2026-11-11). With the client hold fix the loop is not expected any more; this rule is the server-side backstop that does not trust the client.

*Stuck (AC8).* A ball out of the living character's growth range for `StuckTime` (4 s), while it neither moved `StuckProgressStuds` (2) on the ground plane nor came that much closer since the timer started, is put back (`stuck`). The timer starts over on any of those. This leaves normal play alone: a ball next to a standing character is in range; a ball swinging round, coasting or chasing the character keeps moving; a ball held behind an obstacle or lying at the bottom of a cliff does not. The leash stays as it is for balls far away. `StuckFor` shows the timer.

*Slopes (#94 round 2).* The playtest at `30f6833` stood the character still on the 25° test ramp at x ≈ −160 with the 50-stud ball held normally in front of it (3D distance about 41, height difference about 34). The stuck rule fired every 4 to 7 s with `spot=Unsafe` at nearly the same place; only the loop bound slowed it. Two causes, both found in the code and reproduced in `RecoverySlope.spec`:
1. *Stuck reach.* The stuck rule used the growth range (`GrowthRules.inGrowthRange`: at most radius + `GrowthVerticalRange` = 33 studs between root and ball centre in height). A ball held uphill on a 25° ramp is about 34 to 37 studs higher, so it was "out of range" although it was held and did not move. Fix, following the owner rule "the height difference on a slope is not out of reach": for the stuck rule only, a ball also counts as in reach when it is within the growth range on the ground plane and the ground under it is at most `GrowthVerticalRange` + tan(`StuckSlopeDegrees`) × the ground-plane distance away from the character's ground (`RecoveryRules.heldOnSlope`; one ray down from the ball centre, one from the root part, both only when the growth range says no). 40° covers the ramp and the about 35° the hold can hold a ball on; a cliff stays out of reach: at the farthest held distance of a 50-stud ball (31 studs) it allows 34 studs, the test cliff is 70, so a ball at its foot is still put back by the stuck rule or the leash. A ball behind an obstacle is beyond the growth range on the ground plane and stays stuck (AC8). The growth rule itself is unchanged (7.4).
2. *Every candidate `Unsafe`.* `SafeSpot` put the centre radius above the ray hit. On a 25° ramp a ball touching the slope has its centre radius / cos 25° above the hit (2.6 studs more at radius 25), so the centre sat 22.7 studs from the ramp's top face and the overlap check (block parts may reach at most 0.05 into the ball) rejected every spot on the ramp, then the old placement in front was used. The ramp being a rotated block was not the problem: its box is measured in its own frame. Fix: the centre goes radius / (normal Y) above the hit; the support rays reach that much deeper. Obstacles are still checked by box with the same clearance (AC9). With the character facing uphill on the ramp the spot straight in front is out of the growth range by height, so a spot downhill on the ramp (`Around`) is used; facing downhill, `Front`.

*Log (AC2).* In Studio at PlaceId 0 every recovery prints `[BallService] <name> recovery reason=<why> ball=(x, y, z)|none character=(x, y, z) putBack=(x, y, z) spot=<Front|Around|LastSafe|Unsafe> diameter=<d> recoveries=<n> recent=<in window> loop=<at this place in the loop window, this one included>`. `Unsafe` and both bounds also `warn` on live servers.

**After a hit (issue #97).** Modules: `BallController/HoldMath` (client: approach limit and rebound brake), `BallService/HitRules` (pure: same-hit memory, rebound watch), `BallService/AfterHit` (Roblox-facing glue, Studio log) and the Studio-only `BallService/RunAtObstacle` (switch `BallRunAtObstacle`). All numbers are in `Config.Ball` and are proposals until the owner confirms them after the playtest (owner decisions D1 to D3 on #97).

*Cause, proven by the code.* Run A of #34 (`0237df6`, `108db61`) saw the ball leave `LargeObstacle` and `SmallObstacle` at `AssemblyLinearVelocity` 41 to 46 studs/s with the character standing, up to 19.8 (and once 112) studs away, and count again on its way back (`Collisions` 5 → 6, 9 → 13 in about 12 s). `Elasticity` is 0 with `ElasticityWeight` 100, so it is no material bounce. Four things in the code turn a small push into a big excursion and a second collision:
1. *No brake stronger than the hold.* The hold's only limit is `HoldMaxAcceleration` (150 studs/s²); a rolling solid sphere turns 5/7 of a force at its centre into speed (about 107). A ball that leaves the hold point at 46 studs/s needs about 46² / 214 ≈ 9.9 studs to stop; the HoldMath model gives 9.8 studs, settled after 1.5 s (`HoldMath.spec` "#97 cause").
2. *Overshoot on the way back.* Desired speed is 6/s × the distance error, capped at `HoldMaxSpeedFactor` × `WalkSpeed` = 48 studs/s, which is about the speed measured with the character standing. Following that profile needs a deceleration of 6 × speed, more than the 107 available above about 18 studs/s, so a ball coming back from far out overshoots: from 20 studs out by 2.3 (diameter 1.6) and 3.4 studs (diameter 4) inside the hold distance, past the 1.0 at which its surface reaches the `HumanoidRootPart`. It rams the character.
3. *The hold keeps pointing into the obstacle.* The hold direction is the last walking direction, also while the character stands. After any displacement the hold swings the ball back into the same obstacle.
4. *Every return is a new collision.* `ObstacleRules.evaluate` counts a touch whenever the obstacle was not in the previous sample's contacts and the 1 s protection is over. A ball that is more than `CollisionContactMargin` (0.25) away for one sample and back after more than 1 s costs 10 % again.

*Cause, inferred (not proven without Studio).* What gives the ball its first outward speed. Candidates: the character walking into a ball that is pressed against the anchored obstacle (`OwnCharacter` ↔ `OwnBall` collides, and the Humanoid's walk is not force-limited like the hold), so the solver pushes the overlapping ball out sideways or up; a ball meeting the 1-stud `SmallObstacle` off-centre being deflected sideways at hold force; or the overshoot of point 2. The 112 studs fit hold distance (3.5 at diameter 4) + `LeashDistance` (12) + `LeashTime` (2 s) × 48 studs/s ≈ 111.5: a ball moving away at the speed cap until the leash put it back. Why the hold would drive a ball away from a standing character is not explained by the code (the character's velocity is fed forward, so a character shoved by the ball would do it). The run A reports also note that `AssemblyLinearVelocity` reads about twice the speed from positions. The diagnostics below measure this; the fix does not depend on which candidate it is.

*Fix (client, D1).* Two changes in `HoldMath`, both only away from normal pushing (straight pushing at 16, 32 and 40 studs/s gives exactly the old acceleration, `HoldMath.spec`, so growth and #34 AC1 are unchanged):
- *Rebound brake* (`holdAcceleration`). A ball beyond the hold distance whose velocity relative to the character points away from it gets that outward speed braked at `HoldReboundGain` (40/s) × the speed, up to `HoldReboundMaxAcceleration` (1000 studs/s²). It only replaces the normal radial part when it is stronger than both that part and `HoldMaxAcceleration`, so it acts on outward speeds above 150 / 40 = 3.75 studs/s. Everything else, including pressing a ball against an obstacle, keeps `HoldMaxAcceleration`. A far-off ball must not be flung at the character; braking a ball that leaves flings nothing. Model: 46 studs/s now ends 2.2 studs out at 60 fps (2.7 at 30 fps), more than `ReboundSettleStuds` out for about 0.3 s.
- *Approach limit* (`desiredVelocity`). The speed towards the hold distance is at most √(2 × `HoldApproachDeceleration` × error), 60 studs/s², so the ball can always still stop. From 20 studs out the ball now comes at most 0.76 studs inside the hold distance, clear of the root part.
- *Contact hold* (`nextHeldDirection`, rounds 2 and 3, replaces round 1's distance before bearing). While the own ball touches an `Obstacle`, the hold direction is the ball's current bearing from the character, whether the character walks or stands; free again, walking steers as before. The client tests this itself every frame: the box distance from the ball's centre to every `Obstacle`-tagged part it sees (`HoldMath.touchesBox`, list kept current by the tag signals, no allocation per frame), within the ball's radius + `HoldContactMargin` (0.25, the server's `CollisionContactMargin`). It only steers the own ball's hold; collisions, size and the same-hit rule stay on the server. In free field the hold is main's (no contact), so turns are not slowed. Round 4 limits the approach limit and the rebound brake to the time after a contact, and released the contact hold behind a walking character (see "Round 4"). Round 5 removed that release, limits the contact hold to obstacles that cannot be knocked over, and arms on a server move as well (see "Round 5").

The client still sends nothing and decides no size or collision. D3: the small-ball stutter (#93) is not touched.

*Round 1 (playtest at `c755723`).* Three findings, with causes:
1. *Slide along the face, by hand (`LargeObstacle`).* Walking W slightly off −Z into the +Z face, then standing, the ball slid along the face, round the west corner and stayed beside the west face: 4.10 and 4.99 studs beyond the hold distance, 3.85 still at the end (ball (14.5, −30.4), character (19.3, −25.5)). Cause, reproduced in `HoldObstacle.spec` (2D model: kinematic character sliding along ball and box, rolling ball, inelastic contacts; end state ball (14.0, −29.6), character (18.7, −25.0)). While the character presses against the face, its hold point lies inside the obstacle. The character, walking at an angle into the pinned ball, squeezes it sideways along the face (physics). The bearing term (up to `HoldMaxOrbitSpeedFactor` × `WalkSpeed` = 32 studs/s sideways) then drives it round the corner towards the hold bearing, trading distance for bearing. **Collision groups (ADR 0003 rev. 2, extended here).** `BallService` registers four global groups once and applies the table in `Config.Ball.CollisionRules`. The first five rows are the ADR 0003 rev. 2 table. The `OtherCharacters` rows extend it; ADR 0003 itself is unchanged.

| Pair | Collides | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `OwnCharacter` ↔ `OwnBall` | yes | ADR 0003 rev. 2 (pushing) |
| `OwnCharacter` ↔ `Balls` | no | ADR 0003 rev. 2 (walk through foreign balls) |
| `OwnBall` ↔ `Balls` | no | ADR 0003 rev. 2 (concept 7.3 ✅) |
| `Balls` ↔ `Balls` | no | ADR 0003 rev. 2 |
| `OwnCharacter`, `OwnBall`, `Balls` ↔ `Default` | yes | ADR 0003 rev. 2 (world) |
| `OwnBall` ↔ `OtherCharacters` | no | extension: nobody can push or block my ball |
| `OwnCharacter` ↔ `OtherCharacters` | no | extension: nobody can block anyone in the Simulator |
| `Balls` ↔ `OtherCharacters` | no | extension |
| `OtherCharacters` ↔ `OtherCharacters` | no | extension |
| `OtherCharacters` ↔ `Default` | yes | extension (world) |

Why the extension: in the rev. 2 table, foreign characters stay in `Default` on every client. That makes `OwnBall` ↔ `Default` = yes, so another player's character could push or block my ball on my screen. Concept 7.3 ✅ says "Figuren laufen durch fremde Bälle hindurch, niemand kann andere blockieren". The fourth group closes this gap. Development decided this (DM decision in the #33 review, round 1); the concept settles the rule.

Server view (unchanged): every ball is in `Balls`, characters stay in `Default`. Each client's `CollisionController` assigns groups locally from its own view:
- own character parts (including respawns and parts added later) → `OwnCharacter`;
- every part of every other character → `OtherCharacters`. This covers join, respawn, parts added later and parts or models streaming back in; connections are cleaned up on respawn, destroy and leave;
- own ball → `OwnBall`;
- every other ball (also when it streams in again) → `Balls`.

Local `CollisionGroup` changes do not replicate. No part gets `CanCollide = false`, and a source scan in the tests keeps it that way.

**No jumping.** `MovementService` sets `WalkSpeed = Config.Ball.WalkSpeed` and, while `Config.Ball.JumpEnabled` is false, `JumpPower = 0` and `JumpHeight = 0` on every Simulator character; the default touch controls then hide the jump button. `MovementController` turns off the `Jumping` humanoid state on the client, which simulates its own character.

**Camera.** Roblox's third-person camera stays. `CameraController` does two things.

*Fit.* It lifts `Player.CameraMinZoomDistance` to the distance at which the whole ball fits. The camera aims at the character, not at the ball, so the ball is off-axis. `CameraFit.ballReach` is the distance from the camera focus to the farthest point of a ball resting on the ground, `holdDistance` away on any bearing, for a focus between `CameraFocusHeightMin` and `CameraFocusHeightMax` above the ground. The zoom is that reach / sin(narrower half field of view) × `CameraFitMargin` (vertical, or horizontal on a portrait phone). This holds for every ball bearing and camera pitch. The focus height is a range because a big ball's centre is above the focus and a small ball's far below it; using only `radius` for the height difference would be too short for small balls. `CameraFitMargin` is 1.1 (was 1.25 on the old on-axis formula): the bound is already tight for the worst pitch, and more margin only makes the ball smaller on screen. `CameraMaxZoomDistance` rises by `CameraMaxZoomExtra` only if the requirement goes above Roblox's default maximum of 400. At diameter 50 the minimum is 114.0 studs at 16:9 and 212.6 at 9:19.5, so the maximum stays 400. This part runs on ball size, field-of-view and viewport changes only.

*Occlusion.* Roblox's default "Zoom" occlusion (Poppercam) treats the opaque, collidable own ball as an occluder and pulls the camera in front of it, ignoring the zoom minimum. The ball must stay opaque and collidable, so the occluder rules cannot be escaped by changing the part. One render step directly after `Enum.RenderPriority.Camera` pushes the camera back along its look direction to the required distance while it is closer to its focus than that. To keep real walls working, one raycast from the focus towards the wanted camera position limits the push (`CameraWallClearance` before the wall). It excludes the ball folder and all characters: the own ball is what the push looks past, and foreign balls and characters cannot block anyone (concept 7.3 ✅). The step does nothing without a ball or within `CameraPushTolerance` of the required distance, reuses one `RaycastParams` whose filter is updated on player and character events, and allocates no tables per frame. The decision itself is the pure `CameraFit.needsPush` and `CameraFit.pushedDistance`. Invisicam was rejected because it fades the ball itself and also world objects, and the ball has to stay visible.

*After the ball shrinks (owner decision, Marco, 2026-11-09).* Only the minimum drops back. There is no automatic zoom back: the camera may stay far away as long as the player can zoom in with the mouse wheel.

## Consequences

- Server start counts in World 1 grow from 6 to 8 (`BallService`, `MovementService` and `SurfaceService` replace `SimulatorSkeletonService`); client counts from 2 to 6 (`BallController`, `BallGrowthController`, `CameraController`, `CollisionController`, `MovementController` replace `SimulatorSkeletonController`). Lobby and Arena are unchanged and do not load these modules.
- An exploiting owner can move its own ball and its own character freely (it simulates both, including `WalkSpeed`). It still cannot grow faster than `MaxCountedSpeed × fractionPerStud` per second over time, never while airborne or away from snow, and cannot send a size.
- The server writes `Size`/`CFrame` on a client-owned part when it resizes. A short correction may be visible on the owner; check in playtest.
- The ground check is one ray straight down, correct on flat ground. Slopes and ramps need a different check later (kits, #29). Found again in #94 round 2: on the 25° test ramp the growth ray (radius + 0.5 + 2 % of the radius) is shorter than the radius / cos 25° from the centre to the slope, and a ball held uphill is outside `GrowthVerticalRange`, so a ball on a slope does not grow (`Airborne` or `TooFar`). Not changed in #94 (the growth rule belongs to 7.4); open question on #94.
- Characters in the Simulator do not collide with each other either (`OwnCharacter` ↔ `OtherCharacters` = no). Lobby and Arena do not load these modules and keep Roblox defaults.
- Four of the 32 collision groups are used.
- The default occlusion still pulls the camera in for the own ball; the render step then pushes it back in the same frame (see Camera). If the player had zoomed out farther than the minimum, the camera returns only to the minimum while the ball is in the way, because the player's zoom target is private to the default camera.
- When a wall or the ground limits the camera, the push stops before it and the ball may not fit fully. That is deliberate: the camera must not end up behind a wall. Looking up from low down with a big ball is the common case, because the camera then sits at the ground. Every queryable part counts as a wall for the push, including invisible and non-collidable ones (`CanQuery` off excludes a part). The default occlusion ignores non-collidable parts, so a later map with large trigger volumes or decoration between camera and character needs them set to `CanQuery` off.
- The zoom bound assumes the ball rests on the ground at its hold distance. A ball stuck behind an obstacle can be up to `LeashDistance` farther away until the recovery brings it back; only the margin covers that.
- A recovery costs at most 24 candidates × (5 rays + 1 overlap query), only when it happens. On maps with narrow ledges or bridges a big ball finds no supported spot more often and falls back to `LastSafe`; the leash or stuck rule then retries every few seconds until the character stands somewhere safe.
- The minimum zoom also applies to small balls: in zone 1 it is about 12.4 studs in landscape and 23.2 in portrait at fraction 0, because the ball lies well below the focus.
